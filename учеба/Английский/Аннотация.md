# 3 Unit

**The text is titled** "The First Computers".

**The text is devoted to** the history of the first computers, the first analog and digital machines and the scientists who made them.

**At the beginning of the text the author mentions** Vannevar Bush. He built the first analog computer in 1930. **The author tells us** that people used this device during World War II to aim guns.

**The aim of the text is to provide the reader some information on** computers from the 1940s and 1950s. **It describes** the Mark I. Professor Howard Aiken made this first digital computer in 1944. The text also talks about John von Neumann. He had the idea to store instructions in memory using binary code.

**At the end of the text the author draws the conclusion that** the vacuum tube was very important for these early machines. **He mentions** that computers with vacuum tubes were very fast. They could calculate in milliseconds. 

**I found the text** interesting and easy to understand. **I think** it is very useful to know about the people who invented binary code and computer memory.

# Unit 4

The text is titled “Advantages of Computer Data Processing”.

The text is devoted to the main benefits of computer data processing. The text is about how computers help us work faster, more accurately, and store more data.

At the beginning of the text the author explains that computers shouldn't just copy manual work. They should combine human thinking with machine power to solve problems.

The aim of the text is to show four key advantages: accuracy, communication, storage, and speed. Computers reduce errors and share information quickly, for example, in travel booking or office work.

At the end of the text the author draws the conclusion that computers should handle routine tasks so humans can focus on more important things. A good system works well and saves money, helping us live better today.

I found the text easy to understand and useful for my specialty. I think these four advantages explain why computers are used everywhere — in schools, hospitals, and offices.
